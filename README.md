## Abstract

We conducted a field study on bird communities May-August 2023 in four urban and peri-urban greenspaces on the Island of Montreal, Quebec. We asked if there was a relationship between the presence of trails and human activity (quantified by the number of trail users per hour) and avian species richness and diversity in urban greenspaces, and whether bird communities close to (< 25 m) and far from (25 to 100 m) trails differed. We also explored if patterns of bird diversity and composition differed between informal and formal urban greenspaces (referred to here as ‘management status’). We anticipated bird communities far from trails in informal urban greenspaces would be the most diverse and rich in species. We found no evidence to support a significant relationship between three of our predictor variables (human activity, stem density, greenspace management status) and avian species richness or diversity. However, we did find significant positive relationships between the proximity to trails (< 25 m. vs > 25 m) and both avian species richness and diversity. We also compared bird communities close and far from trails in informal and formal urban greenspaces, and found that in informal urban greenspaces, communities close and far from trails are more similar than in formal urban greenspaces. Our results suggest that bird communities are shaped more by the structural presence of trails rather than the number of people using them, within our study system. Possible management outcomes from this work are two-fold: (1) recognizing that trail presence in forested greenspaces provides a novel habitat structure (e.g., shrubby edges) that leads to unique species assemblages and (2) that high traffic but low-density trail networks in greenspaces would facilitate recreational access to people while limiting disturbance to more sensitive, inner forest species in these areas.

## Repository Use

This repository is built using a [targets](https://books.ropensci.org/targets/) workflow and has an `pak` lockfile to preserve package structure. After activating and installing all packages, more instructions [here](https://pak.r-lib.org/reference/faq.html), you can run the workflow using the following code:

```{r, eval = FALSE, echo = TRUE}
library(targets)
tar_make()
```

All required data and associated metadata can be found in the `input/` folder.
